webgpu-audio-bridge — cross-engine notify-cost bench

Isolates the cost of Atomics.notify(read_index) on the consumer hot path by comparing the public SpscRing.pull() against the dev-only _pullNoNotify() shim (same body, notify call omitted). Reports the per-engine delta — the headline input for the 0.7.0 wait-flag wire-format decision.

Node bench (V8 on Windows) measures the delta at ~100 ns. This page exists to check whether that generalizes to JavaScriptCore (Safari + iOS) and SpiderMonkey (Firefox). If Safari shows a much larger delta (because JSC doesn't short-circuit empty wait-lists internally), the wait-flag protocol becomes clearly worth it. If all three engines land near ~100 ns, the protocol's payoff is small everywhere and lane 4 stays unmotivated.

How the per-iteration cost is derived

performance.now() has 5 μs resolution in Chromium and 20 μs in Firefox on a crossOriginIsolated origin (and 1 ms in Safari without --enable-features=… overrides). That is far too coarse to measure a 100 ns per-call delta directly. The harness instead times batches of batchIters push+pull pairs each (default 1000, ≈1.2 ms per batch on V8), divides by batchIters, and treats each batch as one sample. Reporting medians + p99 + p999 + max across batches samples gives a stable per-iteration figure even on engines with clamped clocks.

The push cost is identical between the two paths, so it cancels in the delta. The reported headline is pull_median − noNotify_median: the per-pull cost of the trailing Atomics.notify(read_index, 1) call on a no-waiter steady state.

What to record per engine

Engine string + browser version + OS, then the report block. Paste them into a fresh handoff document or PR description; the comparison across engines is the actual deliverable, not any single engine's number.
