================================================================
Reading
================================================================

The two runs DISAGREE on the sign of the protocol's net effect. Run 1
says the protocol makes per-pull WORSE by 110 ns; Run 2 says it makes
per-pull BETTER by 55 ns. **This is the critical finding for V8: the
per-pull signal is below the bench-to-bench variance.**

What's stable across runs:
  - Protocol overhead (wfClear - noNotify): 55-65 ns. A real, repeatable
    cost. This is the lane-4 Atomics.load + branch + extra function-call
    indirection.
  - Magnitude of the means: ~2.20-2.40 μs across all four paths.
  - Flag-set degenerate case (wfSet) sits within ±150 ns of pull-notify,
    confirming wfSet ≈ noNotify + load + notify as designed.

What VARIES across runs:
  - Notify cost (notify - noNotify): 20 ns vs 165 ns. The signal itself.
  - Protocol savings (notify - wfClear): -45 ns vs +110 ns. Direct
    function of the notify-cost variance.
  - Protocol NET: ±100 ns. Sign-flipping between runs.

**Practical implication.** On V8 Chromium with this harness, the wait-
flag protocol's per-pull effect is statistically indistinguishable
from zero at the single-run resolution. The protocol overhead is real
and repeatable (~60 ns); the savings it recovers are dominated by the
between-run variance of V8's own notify-cost.

Plain reading: the perf case for the protocol on V8 is officially
**neutral-at-best**. There is no run-to-run-stable positive signal.
The 0.7.0 decision cannot be motivated by per-pull perf on V8.

================================================================
What would settle this on V8 alone
================================================================

Two paths:
  - Bigger sample size. 10x the iters (10k batches × 10k iters) takes
    ~5 minutes per run; the SE of the mean drops from ~5 ns to ~0.5 ns
    which should resolve the 20-160 ns signal cleanly.
  - More runs. 10 runs of the current size, computed as
    median-of-medians, would give a similarly tight figure.

Neither is critical at this stage because:
  1. The RT-safety / community-alignment case for the protocol stands
     independently of the per-pull perf signal.
  2. Investigations 1 + 3 together already establish that V8's notify
     is "fast enough that user-code can't beat it." That's the
     headline finding; the exact sign of the net effect doesn't
     change the 0.7.0 framing.

The Firefox + Safari cells (TODO) will check whether the noisy
result is V8-specific or reproduces on other engines. If Firefox /
JSC show consistent net-positive deltas, the protocol case is
stronger; if they also flip sign between runs, the cross-engine
conclusion is "perf is in the noise, ship for hygiene reasons."
